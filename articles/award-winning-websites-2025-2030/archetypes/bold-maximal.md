# Bold Maximal — Effect Palette, Page Recipe, Mid-Page Aliveness

Awwwards jury scores on the bold/maximal line cluster 7.3–7.8, and the one site above 8.0 got there by moving its whole interaction layer into a canvas runtime rather than into readable DOM and CSS. Effect-by-effect repertoire, page shapes, and mid-page mechanics for the line, across Awwwards Site of the Day (SOTD), Site of the Month (SOTM) and Site of the Year (SOTY), FWA, and CSS Design Awards winners 2023–2026. Every parameter below is quoted from a named source (live CSS/DOM/JS read off the shipping build, a Codrops case study, or an award page) and flagged where a site is awarded but its method is undocumented. Parent reference for palette, type scale, fonts, and use cases: [Award-Winning Websites — 2025–2030 Reference](../award-winning-websites-2025-2030.md) §1.4.

### The score reality

Awwwards SOTD jury scores on this archetype cluster **7.3–7.8**; the roster table above carries the verified scores, with Stefan Vitasović's 7.25 (adjacent register) just under the band. The single 8+ scorer, **Lando Norris at 8.18**, is the dark-neon register straddling bold-maximal and immersive-cinematic, and its aliveness lives almost entirely in Rive canvas rather than readable DOM/CSS. The honest picture: **the mid-page techniques that earn 7.6–7.8 on pure bold-maximal are the ceiling of the line**, and the one site that broke 8.0 did it by moving the whole interaction layer into a canvas runtime. This is a bounded-sample generalization over the sampled cohort, not a proof that no higher-scoring bold-maximal site exists.

## Effect palette

### Hover and micro-interactions

**AI-default cliché to ban.** Every button gets the same pale wash (`background: rgba(brand, 0.1–0.2)` fade-in on `:hover` over ~200ms) and every nav item gets the same generic underline. No winner examined fills a button with a washed-out tint of its own color. Winners either **fully invert** the button or drive it with a **physical/dimensional** effect, and they vary the treatment by element class.

#### Buttons and CTAs

**1a. Full-token liquid fill sweeping from an edge** (Cuberto, production plus the Codrops build Codrops credits to it). Two artifacts, one logic. Production site: a pill CTA (`border-radius: 1000px`, `overflow: hidden`) holds a fill layer below it (`transform: translateY(101%)`, `background: currentcolor`, leading edge `border-radius: 50% 50% 0 0`, a curved dome), which rises to `translateY(0)` and flattens on hover, `transition: transform 0.5s cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0, 1)`. The Codrops "Magnetic Buttons" build does the same with a solid circle filler `--button-filler: #ce1352` (vivid magenta, a **full token at zero opacity**), sized `150% × 200%`, parked at `translate3d(0, 75%, 0)`, animated bottom-up on hover over **`0.5s` `Power3.easeOut`** with a `0.4s` retract; sibling demos use `#000` and `#fff` fillers. On the solid variant the fill inverts the button; the icon flips to `color: rgb(0,0,0)`. The fill **edge is not fixed**: Hover.css ships the same idea as named full-color `Sweep` / `Bounce` / `Radial` / `Shutter` variants in all four directions, and bottom-up is the canonical liquid one. **Where it fits.** The primary CTA. The fill runs at full saturation, ~0.5s, directional; the curved leading edge is the signature, and a flat straight wipe reads generic.
Citation: `cuberto.com` live CSS; Codrops Magnetic Buttons (`demo2/button.css`, `buttonCtrl.js`); Hover.css. Caveat: the dome-fill numbers rest on a single live read of Cuberto's interaction CSS.

**1b. Label swap-slide, synced to the fill** (Cuberto/Codrops build). As the fill rises, the rest label tweens out (`opacity: 0, y: -20%`, `0.15s Power2.easeIn`) and a duplicate rolls up into its slot (`y: 100% → 0%`, `0.2s Expo.easeOut`), clipped by `overflow: hidden`. **Where it fits.** Paired with 1a on the primary CTA so the label reads on the new fill; it does not appear on secondary buttons. Corroborated on SOTD `filipporuffini.com`; the second named winner is thin, so treat it as one SOTD plus build docs.
Citation: Codrops Magnetic Buttons (`demo1/buttonCtrl.js`).

**1c. Hover shifts darker, never paler** (design-system corroboration for the anti-tint rule). Where a saturated button recolors on hover, verified systems go to a *darker* shade of the same hue, not a faint tint: Stripe primary `#533afd → #4434d4` plus `translateY(-2px)` lift; GitHub Primer `.btn-primary` rest-token → darkened hover-token in `button.scss`; Tailwind's canonical example `bg-blue-500 → hover:bg-blue-700`. Vercel Geist keeps the primary a full solid `#171717` with no hover wash at all; the faint tint appears only on its *ghost/tertiary* (transparent-at-rest) variant. **Where it fits.** Dark-neon and brand-solid builds where a directional fill would be too much; the verified systems darken the role color or lift the button, never washing it to a tint.
Citation: Stripe/Geist design-token reconstructions; Primer `button.scss`; Tailwind docs; Vercel Geist button.

**1d. Neon accent-swap, JS/Rive-driven face** (Lando Norris). Buttons carry `overflow: hidden` and a Rive-canvas face (`data-btn-rive-rotate="true"`) rather than a CSS fill; nav and label color swaps to the brand neon (`color: var(--color--lime-off)` = `#D2FF00`) on hover. **Where it fits.** Dark-neon builds where the accent color is the whole identity; hover recolors to the single hot hue against near-black. Partly observed: the Rive face is canvas, not inspectable CSS.
Citation: `landonorris.com` live CSS + Awwwards entry.

**1e. Physical clone-storm** (Eloy Benoffi). A "CLICK TO CONNECT" button spawns duplicates as the cursor moves. Codrops verbatim: "duplicates the element twice each time the mouse moves 200px … up to a limit of 200 copies"; the shipped inline JS reads `const TRIGGER_DISTANCE = 200; const COPIES_PER_TRIGGER = 2;` under `/*CONNECT POPUPS*/`, bound to `.about-section`, with no copy cap anywhere (the case study's "limit of 200" restates the pixel step). Clones drop at random absolute positions with `mix-blend-mode: difference` so overlaps collide into new colors; on leave they exit `opacity: 0, scale: 0.6, duration: 0.2, ease: 'back.in(1.7)', stagger: { amount: 0.4, from: 'random' }`. **Where it fits.** One maximalist hero button per page (contact/CTA) where excess is the point; it appears on no repeated UI. [single-source]
Citation: Codrops Eloy Benoffi case study; `eloyb.design` inline JS.

#### Text links and nav items

**1f. Skewed text-roll, mask plus duplicate** (Cuberto). Menu links keep the label in a clipped box with a duplicate stacked below; on hover the pair translates so the clone takes the visible slot (`-105%` on overlay-menu items, `-150%` on the shipped nav rule below), and each layer carries a resolving skew (`::before` and `::after` swap `skewY(0deg) ↔ skewY(7deg)`), giving the roll a diagonal peel. `transition: transform 1.2s cubic-bezier(0.19, 1, 0.22, 1)` on the label, `2s` on the skewed pseudo-layers. The shipped nav rule is `.cb-btn_nav:hover .cb-btn_nav-title span{transform:translateY(-150%) skewY(5deg)}` with the incoming duplicate resolving `skewY(-5deg)`; overlay-menu items run the 7° peel via `.cb-modal_box-nav-item a:hover …span:before{transform:skewY(7deg)}`. **Where it fits.** Full-screen overlay menu items and large nav labels where the roll is legible. The skew is what lifts it above a plain vertical roll.
Citation: `cuberto.com` live CSS, both rules byte-exact.

**1g. Scaling underline / marker draw** (Cuberto, Mat Voyce). Small nav and filter links use a pseudo-element underline that scales in (`.cb-btn_link:hover:after{transform:scaleX(1)}` from `scaleX(0)`) or a clone label at `top: 100%` scaling `0.9 → 1`. This read carries no duration for the underline itself: the `transition: transform 1s cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` quoted alongside it is the **menu-toggle** rule, a different element. A separate read of the same Cuberto `::after` underline records ~`0.3s` on the expo-out curve ([`bento-card.md`](./bento-card.md)). Mat Voyce runs the same quiet register with an active-state swap: `.styles_lineUnderColor:not(.active):hover:after{transform:scaleX(1); transform-origin:left}`, and when active `:hover:after{scaleX(0)}` plus `:hover:before{transform:scaleX(1); transition-delay:.2s}`. Kept deliberately as the **quiet** treatment for secondary links, less than the CTA. **Where it fits.** Utility and footer links, the supporting move that makes 1a and 1f read as the loud ones.
Citation: `cuberto.com` + `matvoyce.tv` live CSS.

**1h. Color swap on footer and utility copy** (Cuberto, Eloy Benoffi). `.cb-contact-terms a:hover{color:rgba(0,0,0,.6)}` and `.floating-foot_copy:hover{color:var(--color--secondary)}`. The lowest-key text hover in the corpus and the only one that lands anywhere near prose.
Citation: `cuberto.com` + `eloyb.design` live CSS.

**1i. CTA overshoot pop** (Cuberto). `.cb-btn_cta:hover{transform:scaleX(1.02);transition:transform .6s cubic-bezier(.34,5.56,.64,1)}`, byte-exact; the sibling overshoot `cubic-bezier(.34,4.56,.64,1)` also ships twice. The overshoot curves are reserved for CTA and round-button scale pops; everything else stays on the expo-out family.
Citation: `cuberto.com` live CSS.

**Color logic in token terms.** Fills resolve to a *full* role color (`accent` / `ink` / `surface`), never an alpha-tinted version of the base. Inversion means swapping `surface ↔ ink`. Accent-swap sends the label to the single signature hue. Where a button recolors instead of filling, it goes darker (1c), not paler. The one legitimate place for a faint low-opacity tint is a ghost or outline variant that starts transparent; there the tint is an emphasis *increase* from nothing, the opposite of washing out a saturated button. Telling detail: the only sub-`1.0` opacity anywhere in the Cuberto build is the *cursor* dropping to `0.2` over interactive elements, never a button fill. The easing family across Cuberto's whole button and link set is one expo-out curve, `cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)` and `cubic-bezier(0.19,1,0.22,1)`, counted at 11 and 8 uses respectively in the live build.

#### The nav bar

**1j. Persistent transparent nav, no solidify, no border** (Cuberto, Lando Norris, Ponpon Mania). What all three share is the surface, not the positioning: `background: rgba(0,0,0,0)`, `backdrop-filter: none`, `border-bottom: 0`, at the top **and** after scrolling. Ponpon's `<header>` reads, live: `position: fixed`, `backgroundColor rgba(0,0,0,0)`, `backdropFilter none`, `border-bottom-width 0px` / `style none`; Lando holds the same fixed transparent bar unchanged through a scroll. Cuberto is the outlier on positioning: `header.cb-navbar` reads `position: static` first-hand from the production DOM, so it never acquires a surface *and* never overlays: the page color changes under it instead ([`../surfaces/navigation-and-hover.md`](../surfaces/navigation-and-hover.md)). Cuberto's bar instead drops a full-viewport curtain, `.cb-navbar-menu { background: rgb(255,255,255); height: 100vh }`, only when the menu opens. None of the three hangs a hairline under the bar. **Where it fits.** The archetype default, on two positioning variants: a fixed transparent overlay (Lando, Ponpon) where the maximal canvas reads through the nav, and a static bar carried by the page flow (Cuberto) where section color does the work an overlay would.
Citation: `cuberto.com`, `landonorris.com`, `ponpon-mania.com` live CSS.

**1k. Frosted-glass surface on scroll** (Aqtos – The BOSS, Blend). When a bold nav does acquire a surface it is translucent and blurred, not an opaque bar: canonical recipe `background: hsl(0deg 0% 100% / 0.5)` plus `backdrop-filter: blur(16px)`, and **no border-bottom**, or a neutral, low-opacity hairline, never a brand color. Both sites are Awwwards-tagged `sticky navigation, blur`. **Where it fits.** Content-dense heroes where the nav stays legible over scrolling media; the surface blurs rather than solidifying. Blur and opacity numbers come from a teaching source; the two winners confirm the family, their exact values are observed.
Citation: Awwwards Aqtos and Blend entries; Josh Comeau backdrop-filter recipe.

**1l. Blend-mode invert, nav reads against the section beneath** (Blind Barber). Nav text and logo carry `mix-blend-mode: difference` so they invert against whatever scrolls under them; the nav's own surface never changes. **Where it fits.** Full-bleed sites alternating light and dark sections, where the nav stays readable with zero surface swap. [single-source at nav level]
Citation: Awwwards Blind Barber; section-level cousin Doel Festival.

### Motion and scroll

**AI-default cliché to ban.** One global `IntersectionObserver` fade-up (`opacity 0→1, translateY 20px, 0.6s ease`) on every section, fire-once, identical everywhere.

**2a. Elastic bounce-in as the house easing** (Ponpon Mania, Maxima Therapy). The signature happy/maximal curve, applied to scale and position pops: Ponpon character bounce `duration: 1, ease: "elastic.out"`, sequence `stagger: i * 1.6`; Maxima sticker pop `scale: 0 → 1, duration: 1, ease: 'elastic.out(1.2, 0.8)'`, CTA MorphSVG at `duration: 0.9` with enter `ease: 'elastic.out(0.8, 0.8)'` and leave `'elastic.out(1.2, 1)'` [single-source for the MorphSVG]; Ponpon's flash effect runs `duration = 0.4 / flashes` over "1–2 flashes". **Where it fits.** Bright-illustrated and playful builds. Two independent sites make it the default overshoot for this line.
Citation: Codrops Ponpon Mania; Codrops Maxima Therapy.

**2b. Scrub-linked scroll, position drives progress, reversible** (Eloy Benoffi, Maxima, Ponpon, Exat). Not fire-once: `ScrollTrigger { scrub: 8 }` on Eloy's hero flower decay (`start: 'top bottom'`, `end: 'bottom-=50% top'`, paths `opacity: 0, duration: 2, ease: 'bounce.inOut'`, `stagger: { each: 0.1, from: 'random' }`), `scrub: 0.6` on his title flow, `scrub: true` on Maxima's parallax (`start: 'top top'`, `end: 'bottom top'`, displacement `20 * self.progress * weight`). Ponpon makes it bidirectional: `ScrollTrigger.create({ start: "top 60%", onEnter: () => tl.play(), onLeaveBack: () => tl.reverse() })`; Exat treats scroll as a structural and state tool, "directly tied to movement… controlled and predictable": stacked panels replace inside pinned sections, prior panel states restore on scroll-back, off-screen motion pauses, and the proximity grid degrades to static on touch. **Where it fits.** Any pinned or hero sequence; scrubbed and reversible is the tier standard, fire-once is the tell.
Citation: Codrops Eloy Benoffi, Maxima Therapy, Ponpon Mania, Exat.

**2c. Graded per-row and random-stagger reveal** (Eloy Benoffi, Ponpon). Reveals are choreographed, not uniform: Eloy's "Selected Work" runs three rows to `yPercent: -300` over `duration: 2` with easing graded per row (`power3.in` / `power2.in` / `power1.in`) and `stagger: { amount: 2, from: 'start' }`; work cards scale in four steps (`1.75→1.5→1.25→1`, `power3.in/inOut/out`) with `stagger: { from: 'random' }` and randomized `transform-origin`. Ponpon's lines enter with per-line random skew. **Where it fits.** Dense grids and lists where a uniform stagger would read mechanical. `from: 'random'` is the maximalist tell.
Citation: Codrops Eloy Benoffi; Ponpon Mania. These params are live in Eloy's inline JS (`yPercent: -300` ×6, `scrub: 0.6` ×1, `from:'random'` ×15, `amount: 0.1` / `0.15` / `0.2` each ×1), so the `technique` tag understates the evidence.

**2d. Shared DOM plus WebGL scene under one scroll** (Ponpon on OGL, Stefan Vitasović on Three.js/R3F, 21 TSI on OGL). Scroll drives a WebGL camera or uniforms, not just DOM transforms: Ponpon `camera.position.y = map(progress, 0.75, 1, -0.4, 3.4)`, `camera.position.z = 5 + velocity * 0.01`, scroll velocity fed into depth; Stefan runs a custom virtual-scroll shared between a WebGL video grid and the DOM, the About page's infinite scroll translating "only the currently visible section"; the grid renders video textures at 60fps through a fragment shader adding an LED overlay plus noise grain, reuses one `PlaneGeometry(width, height, 1, 128)` across meshes, and drops the WebGL layer on mobile for native HTML5 video [single-source]. 21 TSI describes WebGL depth, morphing section transitions, cursor distortion and "scroll-based animations balancing engagement and clarity" with no values. **Where it fits.** The one hero or section carrying real depth. This is the medium that separates 8.5+ scorers, and winners spend it once rather than everywhere.
Citation: Codrops Ponpon Mania, Stefan Vitasović, 21 TSI.

**2e. Page transition as shader blend or masked wipe** (Ponpon, Stefan). Ponpon blends two WebGL scenes via a custom shader driven by GSAP tweens; Stefan, the outlier, uses Framer Motion `AnimatePresence mode="wait"`, `opacity 0→1→0, duration: 0.5, ease: easeQuadInOut` with a noise vertex-displacement along x on the WebGL layer. **Where it fits.** Multi-page maximal sites; the transition is a signature surface, not a cut. Shader blend on two sites; the Framer transition is [single-source].
Citation: Codrops Ponpon Mania; Stefan Vitasović.

**2f. Physics as a secondary tangible touch** (Ponpon, Maxima). Matter.js in a supporting role: Ponpon's About-page bodies and cloud mouse-repulsion (`radius 2`, `strength 1.5`, spring via `springStrength * deltaTime`) [single-source params]; Maxima's careers rope, a Matter rectangle body mapped to text with a canvas rope composited through SVG displacement; its lighthouse wave ripple is the same tangible touch without a physics engine: `duration: 3`, `ease: 'power2.out'`, amplitude `-(pointerSpeed.speedY) * 0.3` across 50 SVG control points with distance-based falloff. **Where it fits.** One playful moment (about or contact), never the primary reading path.
Citation: Codrops Ponpon Mania; Maxima Therapy.

Smooth scroll is near-universal, library varies: Lenis (Maxima, Exat, Cuberto, Eloy, Lando, Ponpon), Locomotive (21 TSI), custom virtual scroll (Stefan); Maxima syncs Lenis to GSAP's ticker through Lenis `onScroll`. Durations, easings, staggers and pin thresholds repeat from a small per-site set rather than being tuned element by element: Cuberto's whole button and link set rides one expo-out curve, Ponpon's one elastic family.

### Text effects

**AI-default cliché to ban.** `SplitText` on every heading with the same `stagger: 0.05, y: 100%, ease: power2.out`, kinetic type firing without a story, the archetype's named failure mode.

**3a. SplitText line or char reveal with a characterful per-unit transform** (Ponpon, Stefan). Not a plain rise: Ponpon splits the About title line by line and enters each from off-right with a random skew, a slight rotation, and an elastic overshoot (code below); Stefan splits per-character with index-scaled timing `1.25s + index*0.025s`, `stagger: delay + index*0.025s`, `ease: easeExpOut` and a masked glass-parallax per segment (`left: (index/total)*100%`, `width: 100/total + 0.01%`). **Where it fits.** The one display headline carrying the page. The random skew and rotation (Ponpon) or the index-scaled expo (Stefan) is what makes it a moment instead of a default. Elastic camp and expo camp do not mix; each site commits to one. The Ponpon line-in as Codrops prints it:

```
from(lines, { x: "100%", skewX: () => Math.random()*50 - 25, rotation: 5,
  opacity: 0, duration: 1, stagger: 0.06, ease: "elastic.out(0.7, 0.7)" })
```

Citation: Codrops Ponpon Mania; Stefan Vitasović; Codrops publishes Ponpon's exact code.

**3b. Variable-font weight/width morph on hover** (Exat, a 1,715-glyph typeface). Display type interpolates `wght` and `wdth` continuously, "smooth and continuous, avoiding hard jumps," tied to a hover region; the case study names hover in the "Design Space" section and the shipped `swPlaygroundTitle` is `mouseenter`-driven. Exat's axis morph is **not** scroll-driven; that mis-attribution is refuted below. Its large numerals ride "slow sine-wave patterns that react to scroll speed", settling when scroll stops and oscillating harder on acceleration, and its `SplitText` use is confirmed with stagger and distance unstated. **Where it fits.** Type-forward sections where the letterform *is* the content. [single-source]
Citation: Codrops Exat microsite.

**3c. Diff text swap** (Eloy Benoffi). GSAP `TextPlugin`, not ScrambleText (see `## Refuted`): hover swaps a label letter-by-letter (`type:'diff', speed:1, preserveSpaces:true, padSpace:true, duration:0.3`) as a location easter-egg, Madrid ↔ Mar del Plata. `SplitText` is named for his hover and transition reveals with params unstated. **Where it fits.** A single interactive label or stat, never body copy. [single-source]
Citation: Codrops Eloy Benoffi.

**3d. Oversized type as the whole interface** (Mat Voyce). Kinetic display type animated well beyond viewport bounds is the site's entire premise, consistent with its Animations subscore of 8.40. A type-designer and animator portfolio of "moving things"; palette cyan `#00D3FF` / lime `#DFFF6B`; Awwwards files it under Animation, Typography, Microinteractions and Music & Sound. "GSAP timelines and WebGL shaders" plus custom cursors circulate as secondhand attributions with no citable primary source and are not asserted. Signature, not supporting: the type moves *as art*, not as a caption. **Where it fits.** Type-designer and agency portfolios where the message is craft itself. The award page carries no durations or easings; they come from a live read of `_next/static/css` and are in `## Mid-page aliveness`.
Citation: Awwwards Mat Voyce entry.

Maxima documents no `SplitText` or per-char reveal; its text reveals are scroll-state-driven only ("text showing… managing each interactive element's state").

Signature versus supporting: one headline runs 3a as the page's identity moment; everything else uses quiet reveals (2c) so the signature stays loud.

### Cursor and pointer

**AI-default cliché to ban.** A ~40px circle-outline dot lagging behind the native arrow on every site, same size everywhere, no state, scaling and filling over any link with `mix-blend-mode: difference`, motion for its own sake. The Awwwards cursor guide warns against exactly this: "Custom cursor is a powerful aesthetic gesture that... deserves respect—don't overdo things," "Turn it off for smaller screens," and it is "less appropriate for corporate and e-commerce projects."

**4a. Contextual state cursor, scale plus mode per target** (Cuberto). A `cb-cursor` fixed follower (`pointer-events: none`) whose `::before` rescales and re-modes by what it is over: `-active` (mousedown) `scale(0.2)`, `-pointer` `scale(0.3)`, `-opaque` `scale(0.5)`, `-lg` `scale(1.15)`, `-text` `scale(0.8)` with `transition-duration: 0.2s` as it becomes a text container, `-icon` `scale(0.9)`, `-menu` `scale(0.55)`; a `cb-cursor-media` mode swells into a `~324px` circular media/text container on hover. LERP smoothing amount `0.2`; over interactive elements it scales `×4` and drops to `opacity: 0.2`. Driven by Cuberto's open `mouse-follower` library on GSAP v3, documented defaults `speed: 0.55`, `ease: 'expo.out'`, magnetic `stickDelta: 0.15`, velocity-skew `skewing` (`skewingDelta 0.001`, max `0.15`), `showTimeout: 20`ms, `hideTimeout: 300`ms. The library carries per-element state (text, icon, image or video inside the cursor), is credited in production on Cuberto, Potion, Wickret, Papumba and Safe Security, and a separate `cursor-magnetic-demo` pairs it with a magnetic effect on jQuery plus GSAP v3. **Where it fits.** The primary interaction cue where the design hides the native cursor; the *state changes* carry the meaning, the lag alone does not.
Citation: `cuberto.com` live CSS; Cuberto `mouse-follower` README; Codrops Magnetic Buttons `cursor.js`.

**4b. Magnetic pull on targets** (Cuberto/Codrops build). The target translates toward the cursor at `0.3×` the offset from its own center, triggered when the pointer enters within `element.width × 0.7`, LERP-smoothed at amount `0.1`; the inner label counter-moves at `0.6×` for parallax depth. **Where it fits.** CTAs and icon buttons already carrying one of the fill or swap mechanics (1a, 1b). Magnetism is the carrier, not the effect itself.
Citation: Codrops Magnetic Buttons (`demo1/buttonCtrl.js`); Awwwards magnetic-button inspiration.

**4c. Concept-bound cursor, glyph or color-invert, scoped** (Figma Config 2024, Waaarhol, Le Studio Digital). The cursor carries the site's idea rather than a generic dot: Figma Config swaps the pointer for Config glyphs over pull-quotes and **scopes it to one zone**, native pointer everywhere else; Waaarhol and Le Studio Digital invert the color of whatever content sits beneath. Lux Expression's dot is a small yellow one rhyming with its infographics; Mat Voyce documents "dynamic cursor states and media controls" and Obys an Awwwards custom-cursor element (OHMY/Obys) tagged `cursor` and `animation` only, both observed. **Where it fits.** Where the custom cursor *means* something: tied to the brand mark or reacting to the content beneath it, and scoped rather than globally decorative. Color-invert on two sites; glyph-swap [single-source].
Citation: Figma Config 2024 branding blog; Awwwards Le Studio Digital, Waaarhol.

**4d. Cursor-driven WebGL distortion** (21 TSI). The cursor warps the WebGL layer instead of drawing a DOM cursor. **Where it fits.** Fully WebGL heroes. [single-source, qualitative]
Citation: Codrops 21 TSI.

**4e. Deliberate native cursor** (Lando Norris). No custom cursor element at all: `body { cursor: auto }`, no `[class*="cursor"]`; the motion budget goes to WebGL, Rive, and scroll instead. **Where it fits.** Pages already dense with motion; a custom cursor on top of that budget is subtractive. A defensible choice, not an omission, and the only verified global default in the line: Locomotive's case study mentions no cursor, consistent with a default but unproven, and Figma Config's is scoped rather than global.
Citation: `landonorris.com` live CSS.

### Loader effects

**AI-default cliché to ban.** A centered or bottom-right `0→100%` counter on a flat brand background, then a full-height curtain wipe, on a static hero with nothing to preload and timed rather than load-bound: "A timed preloader that hides already-rendered content is actively lying to the browser about when the page is ready… your LCP gets inflated by the full duration of the preloader." A real-load preloader dismisses on `window.load`.

**5a. Preloader that becomes a page element** (Eloy Benoffi). The loader animates a stripped-down version of the hero while a loading bar grows; the hero scales down and **the loading bar becomes the navbar**. The intro is not discarded; it resolves into the live UI. **Where it fits.** Waits that read as assembly rather than a gate. [single-source]
Citation: Codrops Eloy Benoffi, verbatim: "The whole section is scaled down while a loading bar grows, which later becomes the navbar."

**5b. Intro as first act of the narrative** (Ponpon Mania). No generic spinner: the homepage opens on a simple welcoming composition built on a music-player metaphor (chapters are albums, panels are songs), and the opening choreography is the comic's first beat, GSAP-timeline driven. **Where it fits.** Narrative and comic microsites where the load *is* scene one.
Citation: Codrops + Awwwards Ponpon Mania.

**5c. Asset-gated counter with character** (GT America / Klim, Black Messiah, Naya Studio). A `0→100` count exists to preload multi-MB hero media, and the numerals carry the concept rather than being a plain digit: hand countdown (GT America), brush-painted numbers (Black Messiah), counter plus custom cursor (Naya), a plain "Counter loading animation" (digitaldesigner.cool), and B14's vector clock, time-based rather than 0–100. Chunky `steps(n)` progression is the documented mechanic. **Where it fits.** Only where the hero genuinely ships heavy media; on every named site the counter is typographic rather than a naked percentage. Counter family on two-plus named sites.
Citation: Awwwards loading-animations round-up; Codrops preloader pattern.

**5d. Physics or draw-on logo assemble** (Obys "Design Education Series", Radiant Studios, Wonderland). The wait resolves the brand mark: Obys drops jumbled letters via Cannon.js/Three.js physics that settle into the logo; Radiant animates the logo on; Wonderland runs a multi-colored logo-eraser. **Where it fits.** Brand and agency sites where the mark is the payoff; the loader *is* the logo reveal. Assemble/draw family on two-plus named winners.
Citation: Codrops Design Education Series; Awwwards round-up.

Concept and character loaders on named sites whose individual award status is unverified: Femme & Fierce ("Lipstick loading animation"), SIROPPE ("Cute dancing loading animation"), Poolside FM ("Retro loading ascii code"), The Geek Designer ("8bit loading animation"), Michelberger Hotel ("colorful loading transition"), ITG (walking dachshund), Crazy About Eggs (chicken), Tolia (melting ice-cream), Humans (3D characters).

**5e. Deliberate restraint over a curtain** (Locomotive, SOTM). A named winner chose a light letter-shuffle transition plus a pixel lazy-loader ("we wanted something subtle yet impressive… the 'letter shuffle' transition worked well with our pixel lazy loader effect"), stated it "avoided excessive animations and gadgets", and held to two typefaces in four styles, with no full-screen counter and curtain. **Where it fits.** Content and editorial-leaning maximal sites where a heavy intro would tax LCP. The restraint here is a light loader rather than an absent one; a decorative curtain on a static hero is the tell.
Citation: Awwwards Reinventing Locomotive case study.

### Composition

The heart of the line: winners run a *different* interaction per element class, held together by one shared easing, color, and timing grammar so the set reads as one voice.

#### Cuberto — variety unified by one expo-out easing family

| Element class | Its own interaction | Shared grammar |
|---|---|---|
| Primary CTA | rising dome fill → inversion (1a), `0.5s cubic-bezier(0.4,0,0,1)` | pill geometry, `overflow:hidden`, full-color fill |
| Overlay menu item | skewed text-roll `-105%` + `7°` peel (1f), `1.2s cubic-bezier(0.19,1,0.22,1)` | expo-out family |
| Nav / footer / utility link | scaling underline or clone `scaleX` / `scale 0→1` (1g), `cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)` at ~`0.3s` | expo-out family |
| Cursor | contextual state-scale (4a), `expo.out`, `speed 0.55` | expo-out family, same overshoot feel |
| Nav bar | `position: static`, fully transparent + white full-bleed curtain on open (1j) | no border, no surface at any scroll depth |

What makes it cohere: **four visibly different mechanics** (fill, roll, underline, cursor) all ride the **same expo-out curve** (`cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)` ≈ `(0.19,1,0.22,1)`, counts under Color logic above) and the same rule that fills resolve to *full* role colors. Loud versus quiet is deliberate: the CTA fill and menu roll are the signatures, the underline is the supporting hush. That hierarchy, not sameness, is the unity.

#### Ponpon Mania — variety unified by the comic/music metaphor

| Element class | Its own interaction | Shared grammar |
|---|---|---|
| Characters / props | elastic bounce + mouse-repulsion physics (2a, 2f) | `elastic.out`, comic "breath" |
| Display headline | SplitText per-line skew/rotation (3a) | elastic overshoot |
| Scroll sections | WebGL camera map + velocity depth (2d), reversible (2b) | scroll = playhead |
| Navigation | music-player metaphor — chapters as albums, panels as songs | rhythm / narrative |
| Page transition | two WebGL scenes shader-blended | scene-to-scene, cinematic |

What makes it cohere: every mechanic (physics, type, scroll, nav) serves one story, a DJ sheep's comic. The elastic overshoot is the through-line; scroll is treated as a playhead, nav as a track list. Variety never reads as randomness because each effect answers the metaphor. Maximalism coheres when every kinetic decision serves one narrative, not when every element repeats one trick.

**The transferable rule.** Each winner runs one easing family, expo-out or elastic, never both. One fill logic, full role colors, never tints. One loud signature per element class, CTA ≠ link ≠ image ≠ nav, with secondary elements running quieter variants of the same grammar. Unity comes from the shared curve, the color rule, and the loud/quiet hierarchy, not from cloning a single hover.

### Anti-signals — absent from every winner examined

- **Pale-tint button fill.** No winner fills a saturated button with a washed-out `rgba(brand, 0.1–0.2)` tint on hover. Verified alternatives: full-token directional fill (Cuberto `#ce1352`, `Power3.easeOut`), darker same-hue shift (Stripe `#4434d4`, Primer, Tailwind `blue-700`), full-solid-no-wash (Geist `#171717`), accent-swap (Lando), or physical (Eloy). The faint tint is correct **only** on ghost and outline variants that start transparent; porting that logic onto a primary CTA is the AI tell. The lazy source is shadcn's `hover:bg-primary/90` and `/10` strings.
- **One universal hover on every element.** Winners vary the mechanic by element class. A single fill-sweep reused on buttons *and* links *and* cards is the tell.
- **Solid nav bar with a contrasting brand-colored border-bottom on scroll.** Found on **no** named winner. Lando and Ponpon keep a fixed transparent overlay through scroll and Cuberto a `position: static` transparent bar; when a bold nav does take a surface it frosts (1k) or blend-inverts (1l), with **no border or a neutral low-opacity hairline**. A brand-colored underline appears only in themes and tutorials, and even there as a per-link hover accent, not a nav-wide scroll state.
- **Fire-once uniform fade-up.** Winners scrub and reverse (2b) and grade or randomize stagger (2c); a global `IntersectionObserver` fade-up on every section is the default.
- **Kinetic type with no story.** The archetype's named failure mode: `SplitText` firing everywhere. Winners spend one signature headline (3a) and keep the rest quiet.
- **Lagging-dot cursor with no state, left on at mobile.** A follower dot the same size everywhere with magnetism bolted onto everything is a concept-less clone of `mouse-follower`. Winners give it contextual state (4a), bind it to the concept (4c), or drop it deliberately (4e), and kill it on small screens.
- **`0→100` counter plus full curtain on a static hero.** The exact combo lands on no single named winner; it inflates LCP by hiding rendered content. Winners tie the intro to real preloading (5c), resolve it into the UI (5a) or the logo (5d), carry the narrative (5b), or show documented restraint (5e).

### Named page shapes

Three page shapes recur across this line. Each is an ordered skeleton, not a template; the medium (WebGL, kinetic type, illustration) swaps by brief.

#### Shape A — Specimen Tour

**Fingerprint.** A product or typeface names itself in an oversized hero, then marches through one-capability-per-section feature blocks, each a self-contained demo, closing on a single acquisition CTA reprised from the hero. Sections are peers, not a funnel that tightens; the proof is distributed: every section is a live demo of the thing. Climax is a spectacle section mid-to-late (a 3D type rotation, a proximity grid); rest is the closing credits. No card grid, no testimonial wall. Fits typeface launches, design-tool marketing, product microsites, single-artifact sites.

Skeleton (Exat, winner-verified nav order plus Codrops anatomy). Intensity is a comparative 1–10 estimate of attention load (defined in [Composition Chains](../winners/composition-chains.md)):

1. Hero: wordmark + one-line thesis (attention, intensity 8) → seam: scroll cue into demo
2. About / thesis expansion (understanding, 4)
3. Type tester / interactive demo (understanding + proof, 6)
4. Extensive Character Set: proximity glyph grid (proof, 7)
5. Design Space: variable-axis exploration (proof, 6)
6. Stylistic Sets / OpenType Features (proof, 5)
7. Language Support (proof, 4), the quiet before
8. Closing CTA + footer credits (close, 6)

Section count 7–9, length ~10–14vh, climax at the interactive demo and glyph grid, rest at Language Support then footer. The live nav carries the same seven section labels.

#### Shape B — Agency Statement

**Fingerprint.** A plain-language hero states what the studio does in one declarative sentence, then proof (featured work) leads immediately, services explain, a thought/blog strip adds texture, and an oversized contact reprise closes into a designed footer. This is the only shape in the line that runs a real attention → understanding → proof → close funnel; maximalism lives in the interaction layer (dome-fill CTA, skewed roll, contextual cursor) over an otherwise legible grid. Fits studios, agencies, freelance portfolios, product companies with a services story.

Skeleton (Cuberto, shipped copy plus winner-verified interaction CSS):

1. Hero: "Digital design & development agency" + subhead + "What we do" (attention, 7) → seam: scroll into work
2. Featured projects: large project tiles (proof, 8, the climax) → "View all projects"
3. Our services (understanding, 5) → "View all services"
4. Blog (texture, 4) → "Visit our blog"
5. "Have an idea?" contact reprise (close, 7) → "Tell us"
6. Footer: dual addresses, contact, index links (close, 5)

Section count 5–6, length ~8–11vh, climax at Featured projects, rest at Blog then footer.

#### Shape C — Narrative Chapters

**Fingerprint.** A narrative loader resolves into a cover screen that is scene one, never a curtain dropped over a static hero. A wordmark, a byline, a "read now" entry, a chapter index, and an EN/FR toggle sit over a full-bleed interactive canvas. Entering routes into chaptered WebGL scenes with panel-camera moves and physics micro-interactions; an About page runs a conventional scroll. The page is *read*, not scanned. Fits entertainment microsites, comic and story brands, campaign narratives, interactive editorial.

Skeleton (Ponpon Mania, winner-verified cover plus Awwwards shipped anatomy):

1. Cover / menu: wordmark, byline, "read now", chapters/about nav, EN|FR (attention, 9) → seam: "read now" enters the reader
2. Chapter selection interface (understanding, 6), album-cover chapter nav per Codrops
3. Chapter scenes: WebGL panels, camera perspective shifts, physics mouse interactions, pagination (proof + spectacle, 9, climax) → panel transitions between scenes
4. About: scroll-based animation (rest/close, 4)
5. Legal notice / cookie microcopy (chrome, 2)

Length varies by chapter count; climax is the chaptered reader, rest is About.

**Cross-shape note.** Single-scroll portfolios (Eloy Benoffi) are Shape B compressed to one scroll: hero (loader → nav) → Selected Work → horizontal project sliders → About/Connect clone-field spectacle → footer. It reads as Agency Statement without the services and blog middle.

### Hero architectures

#### H1 — Wordmark-as-hero

The brand or typeface name is the largest object on the fold, rendered as an art asset (WebGL-illustrated, or the typeface set in itself), while the semantic `<h1>` is a *small* credit line. Nav is a transparent fixed bar; a scroll or entry cue sits low.

**Ponpon Mania** (winner-verified): the big "PONPON MANIA" wordmark renders in `canvas.webgl-canvas`, full-bleed, `position:absolute; inset:0; pointer-events:auto`; the DOM `<h1>` is the 9px credit "by Justine Soulié and Patrick Heng" in Libre Franklin 700, color `rgb(254,236,227)` = `#FEECE3` on a `rgb(23,23,23)` = `#171717` base. `<header>` is `position:fixed; background:rgba(0,0,0,0); backdrop-filter:none; border-bottom:0`. Entry CTA "read now" is a dark pill, `border-radius:42px; padding:10px 25px 12px; background:#171717; color:#FEECE3`, all four values confirmed byte-exact.

**Exat** (winner-verified): hero wordmark "Exat" set in the Exat variable typeface itself. The live page ships **zero `<h1>`**; the only heading is the `<h2>` thesis line, black on a transparent/white base; the anchored section nav doubles as the hero's table of contents. `document.fonts` loads Exat Variable plus Exat Condensed and Wide at weights `200, 300, 400, 500, 700, 800, 900`; `600` is absent and the stated 200–900 range holds.

**Mat Voyce** (winner-verified): "Mat" and "Voyce" split across `<h4>` blocks set in F37 Judge (Extended plus Condensed widths), on cream `rgb(255,254,248)` = `#FFFEF8`.

The hero's one entrance beat is the 3a `SplitText` line-in; Codrops' exact code is quoted under Text effects in `## Effect palette`.

#### H2 — Pitch-statement hero

A declarative plain-language `<h1>` sentence names what the company does; a subhead expands it; a low-key CTA labels the next move; proof sits immediately below the fold. Restraint in the copy, maximalism in the motion.

**Cuberto** (shipped, live): `<h1>` "Digital design & development agency"; subhead "We help companies build scalable digital products with thoughtful design systems and carefully crafted development."; CTA "What we do"; "Featured projects" is the next section.

#### Cuberto — 7.37 SOTD, agency / prose register

#### Exat Typeface — 7.73 SOTD, type-specimen register

The richest mid-page arsenal of any site examined, because a type specimen has no photography to hide behind; every content section performs. All winner-verified from `layouts/*/script.min.js`; every identifier survived minification as `window.R2Methods.*`. The code distributes the mechanics across files as `proximityLetters` + `expanders` + `tooltips` in `glyph_black_section`, `arhitectBlock` + `circleAnimation` in `glyph_section`, and `aboutBigTitle` + `typeDemo` in `about_section`.

1. **Cursor-proximity glyph field (`proximityLetters`).** A grid of glyph `.frame` elements. On `mousemove`, each glyph's distance to the cursor is computed, then mapped through **7 distance rings** to *both* a variable-font weight and a color, smoothly interpolated between rings:

   | ring | `--fw` | `--color` |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 (far) | 42 | `#0000cb` |
   | 2 | 70 | `#2546FF` |
   | 3 | 110 | `#5C92FF` |
   | 4 | 138 | `#FFCE2E` |
   | 5 | 160 | `#FFAE00` |
   | 6 | 200 | `#FF6200` |
   | 7 (near) | 228 | `#FF0B00` |

   All seven rings read verbatim from the shipped script as `{fw:42,color:"#0000cb"}…{fw:228,color:"#FF0B00"}`. Weight is linearly interpolated by distance (`S()`); color is hex-lerped (`I()` / `$()`). Per-glyph CSS custom props `--fw`, `--color`, `--opacity` are written in a `requestAnimationFrame` loop throttled to 16ms (`e-l>16`) and **gated to viewport**: `ScrollTrigger.create` sets `onEnter → g=!0`, `onLeave → g=!1`. This is the mid-page centerpiece. (winner-verified)
2. **Field activation and teardown.** Entering the section tweens a master `v.val` from 0→1 (`gsap.fromTo(v,{val:0},{val:1,duration:.4,ease:"power3.out"})` on `mouseenter`, the field "waking up"), and `gsap.to(v,{val:0,…})` on `mouseleave` reverses it. A `scroll` listener recomputes glyph centers via `F()` so the field stays live while scrolling through. (winner-verified)
3. **About headline that re-spins bidirectionally (`aboutBigTitle`).** `SplitText(o,{type:"lines"…})`; each line gets its own ScrollTrigger: `onEnter → gsap.to(e,{rotateX:-360, duration:1, ease:"back.out"})`, `onEnterBack → rotateX:0`. The headline flips a full turn every time it enters view, forward or backward. (winner-verified)
4. **Per-char scrubbed title rise (`glyphBlackTitle`).** `SplitText` by chars, `y:"-105%" → 0`, `stagger:.1`, `ease:"power1.inOut"`, welded `scrub:!0` with `start:"top 100%", end:"50% 50%"`. (winner-verified)
5. **Cursor-tracked rotating figure (`arhitectBlock`).** A media element rotates to the cursor's angle via `atan2`, snapped to 45° increments (`o=45*Math.round(o/45)`), driven by a rAF lerp loop, active only while the section is in view. (winner-verified)
6. **Viewport-gated infinite spin with entry speed-ramp (`circleAnimation`).** Circles spin `{rotate:360, …repeat:-1, paused:!0}`, paused and played by ScrollTrigger enter/leave; on entry a `timeScale` tween ramps 10→1 (`gsap.fromTo({val:10},{val:1,duration:10,ease:"expo.out",…timeScale})`), so the spins decelerate as the section arrives. Plus a pinned sticky track (`pin:i`). (winner-verified)
7. **Interactive type tester and variable-font title morph (`typeTester`, `swPlaygroundTitle`).** Sliders write `--ff` / `--fs` / `--fw` / `--lh` / `--ls` live through five `setProperty` calls; color swatches set `--background` on the section. Hovering list items fires `mouseenter → gsap.to(i,{fw,wd,x,ease:"power2.out",onUpdate:…--fnt "wght"/"wdth"…--x})`, tweening a display title's `font-variation-settings` and x-offset. (winner-verified)
8. **Scrubbed clip-path wipe on media (`typeDemo`).** `clipPath` polygon animated `ease:"none"`, `scrub:!0`, `start:"top 50%", end:"bottom 50%"`. (winner-verified)
9. **Accordion plus repositioning tooltip (`expanders`, `tooltips`).** `gsap.from(inner,{height:0, duration:.75, ease:"power2.inOut"})` plays and reverses on click; a `.tooltip` repositions to each glyph on `mouseenter` via `translate(${n}px,${c}px)`, showing the letter plus its style variation. (winner-verified)

Libraries, all six `<script>` tags in the HTML: gsap + ScrollTrigger + ScrollToPlugin + SplitText + lenis + splide.

4. **CSS `:hover` count = exactly 4** in the shared CSS: `.floating-foot_copy`, `.w-lightbox-control`, `.w-lightbox-inactive`, `a`. The only text-hover is `.floating-foot_copy:hover{color:var(--color--secondary)}`. (winner-verified)

#### Lando Norris — 8.18 SOTD and Site of the Year 2025, dark-neon crossover

The outlier score, and instructive: **its aliveness is not in the CSS.** 206 Rive references in the HTML and 307 in the bundle, exactly **13 `:hover` rules in *each* of the two shared CSS files**, none on `h1`–`h6` or text, and the *only* bezier in either file is `cubic-bezier(.19, 1, .22, 1)`. The interaction layer (hover responses, panel motion, the accent life) is authored inside **Rive** state machines rendered to canvas, with **Lenis** smoothing (12 refs in the HTML, 75 in the bundle) and **ScrollTrigger** (22 refs in `lando-by-OFF+BRAND.js`, a 1.45MB bundle) sequencing. The top scorer bought its extra points by moving choreography out of the DOM into a runtime that CSS and JS inspection cannot read.

### Hover on text

**The tier ships almost no hover on non-interactive text: body prose, static headings, standalone numbers. Every text-hover found targets an *interactive* text element.** A firm, cross-site result, confirmed across every CSS read: Cuberto (nav / link / footer), Mat Voyce (`lineUnderColor` control), Eloy (`floating-foot_copy` only), Lando (zero text or heading hover), Exat (a control retargeting a heading, not hover *on* the heading).

What exists, by form (the rules themselves are quoted in `## Effect palette`):

- **Skewed vertical roll on nav labels** (1f): the loud register, Cuberto.
- **Scaling underline / marker draw** (1g): the *quiet* register, on utility, footer, and nav links; Cuberto and Mat Voyce.
- **Color swap on footer and utility copy** (1h): the lowest-key text-hover and the only one that lands near prose; Cuberto and Eloy.
- **Variable-font morph driven by control hover.** Exat's `swPlaygroundTitle`: hovering a list *control* tweens a display title's `font-variation-settings "wght"/"wdth"` and x via `power2.out`. The characterful text-hover exists, but it is a control retargeting a heading. (winner-verified)

What is **absent**: no color sweep along a paragraph, no per-char rise on hovering a heading, no weight or optical-size shift on hovering body copy, no background-highlight on prose, no hover response on standalone numbers. Static reading text is left alone; Exat's cursor-proximity effects are a *field* mechanic on a glyph grid, not text-hover on prose. **If a build wants mid-page life, hover-on-prose is not where the tier puts it.**

### Re-fire behavior

The law *content persists, decor reverses* holds at the agency register and is **partly inverted at the type-specimen register**.

**Obeys the law: Cuberto, agency.** Content reveals use `IntersectionObserver {once:!0}`, which plays once and stays put. Parallax uses `scrub:!0` timelines (`top bottom → bottom top`), which reverse on every pass. Clean separation, two engines. (winner-verified)

**Decor that re-fires every pass, all sites:**

- Cuberto parallax: `scrub:!0`. (winner-verified)
- Exat `typeDemo` clip-path wipe: `scrub:!0`; `arhitectBlock` cursor-rotation and `circleAnimation` spins: viewport-gated, re-arming on `onEnter` / `onEnterBack`. (winner-verified)
- Eloy work-title rows: `scrub:0.6`. (technique, live-verifiable)
- Ponpon panel loops: continuous while in view. (technique)
- Mat Voyce bg marquee: `linear infinite`. (winner-verified)

**Content-text reveals that re-fire: the register-dependent inversion.** Exat welds even its headline and title reveals to scroll: `aboutBigTitle` re-spins each line a full `rotateX:-360` on `onEnter` and back to `rotateX:0` on `onEnterBack`, replaying bidirectionally; `glyphBlackTitle`'s per-char rise is `scrub:!0`, so scrubbing up or down replays it. (winner-verified)

A type specimen treats its titles as *reversible decor welded to scroll*, because on that register the type performing repeatedly is the content. An agency site treats its content copy as *fire-once and persist*, because re-triggering a value proposition on every scroll-back reads as jitter. **The law is register-conditional, not universal.**

Practical read for the dead-prose problem: the merely-good build's failure is having *neither* channel mid-page: no scrubbed decor welded to scroll, and content that faded once and then sits inert with nothing else moving. The tier always runs at least the scrubbed or looping decor channel through the prose zone (parallax, marquee, viewport-gated spin, panel loops), even when content reveals are fire-once.

### Mid-page anti-signals

- **No hover on static prose or headings.** Reading text is inert; hover life is spent only on interactive text: nav, links, controls. (cross-site, winner-verified)
- **No ambient churn as the primary life.** Winners spend energy on scroll and input, not background drift. Idle is one channel (Mat Voyce's single 10s marquee, Ponpon's panel loops), and Exat and Cuberto gate even that to viewport presence to keep it cheap and contrastful. No perpetual decorative particle drift over content. (winner-verified)
- **No uniform fire-once fade-up on every section as the *only* motion.** The tier either grades the stagger (`from:'random'`, per-row eased `power3/2/1.in`) or welds a scrubbed or looping channel through the zone. A single `IntersectionObserver` fade-up per section with nothing else moving is the merely-good build's tell. (winner-verified via Cuberto's dual-engine split; Eloy's grading)
- **No cloned universal hover.** The mechanic varies by element class: nav rolls (`skewY`), links draw (`scaleX`), cards scale and swap media, CTAs pop (overshoot bezier). One hover reused on buttons *and* links *and* cards is off-tier. (Cuberto, winner-verified)
- **No effect that runs off-screen.** Every rAF and infinite loop found is gated to viewport: `onEnter` / `onLeave` toggles in Exat's `proximityLetters`, `circleAnimation`, and `arhitectBlock`, plus ScrollTrigger play/pause. Off-screen motion is not left burning CPU. (winner-verified)
- **The 8+ outlier's method is not portable via CSS.** Lando's extra points come from a Rive canvas runtime, not richer DOM/CSS. A build that cannot ship Rive should not expect to reach 8.0 by adding CSS hover states; the ceiling for readable-CSS bold-maximal is ~7.7. (winner-verified absence)
