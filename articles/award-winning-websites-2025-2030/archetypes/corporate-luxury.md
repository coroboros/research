# Corporate Luxury — Effect Palette, Page Recipe, Mid-Page Aliveness

Corporate-luxury winners ceiling at ~7.64 overall on the Awwwards jury because restraint costs Usability points, and their middles stay alive on a reversible scrubbed decor bed running under fire-once content reveals. Effect palette, page recipe, and mid-page aliveness for the corporate-luxury archetype. Parent reference: [Award-Winning Websites — 2025–2030 Reference](../award-winning-websites-2025-2030.md).

The unifying trait is **inherited restraint made legible**: a custom or premium serif at display, a two-tone warm-neutral or monochrome palette, generous whitespace, long unhurried motion. Unlike the experimental line, this one shares a conventional page grammar: the winners diverge on register (flat editorial maison, argument long-scroll, cinematic engine), not on structure.

### The score reality

The line's ceiling is **~7.64 across the four read at source level; the two carried attributions (Longines 7.65, Omega 7.32) are unread and excluded.** Cartier 7.64, Son Daven 7.62, Depo Luxe 7.62, Brunello 7.19, Delvaux an Honorable Mention with no jury number displayed. The structural reason is legible in the sub-scores: **Usability caps every site at 7.02–7.17**. Restraint, hidden gestures, slow reveals, and pageless flows read as friction to the jury, and no amount of craft buys it back. Cartier, the corpus canonical, scores its lowest on Usability (7.17) and highest on Creativity (7.97).

The ceiling holds for the *overall* score only. Sub-scores and the developer track cross 8.0 freely: Son Daven's Creativity is **8.15** and its Developer Award **8.09**, Cartier's Animations/Transitions is **9.00**, Depo Luxe's Animations/Transitions **8.40**. Chasing 8.5 overall inside this archetype is chasing a number the archetype cannot mint; the target is the craft ceiling.

## Effect palette

### Hover and micro-interactions

The AI-default: every button gets a `::before` sliding `rgba(accent, 0.1–0.15)` across the CTA; nav items get an underline-grow; the scrolled nav bar takes a frosted-white plate with a contrasting `border-bottom` in the brand accent. One trick on every element class.

**Not one winner in this corpus fills a button with a pale or washed tint on hover, and not one hangs a different-colored `border-bottom` under the nav.** The button repertoire is roll, invert-to-full-token, line-draw, or nothing.

**a. Label roll-swap, no color change.** Two copies of the label stacked in an `overflow:hidden` clip, the hover copy pre-offset one line below at `translateY(100%)`; on hover both roll up together (`translateY(-100%)`), current out the top, duplicate into place. Delvaux: `transform .2s cubic-bezier(.65,0,.35,1)` `[CSS]`. Son Daven stages the same dual-label roll on its pill CTA `[JS structure verified; driver unverified]`. **Where it fits.** Buttons reading mechanical and precise with no color event, the default primary-CTA move for editorial maisons.

**b. Invert to the full section token, never a tint.** Son Daven's pill `.btn_bg` takes `background-color` = the full `#2c2824` / `#a89474` section token, geometry extended `-4px` past the label, with a 2px outline in the section background color `[CSS]`. Depo Luxe reserves true invert (`.btn:not(.--full):hover{background-color:var(--color);color:var(--bg)}`, `.3s ease-out`) and quarantines it to the cookie banner `[CSS]`, evidence that the invert instinct is deliberately kept off site chrome. **Where it fits.** A single hero CTA carrying weight; the fill resolves to the full token, never `rgba(token, 0.1)`.

**c. Line-draw / stroke-draw instead of fill.** Brunello Cucinelli's icon-CTAs draw an SVG ring on hover: `.o-icon--circle{stroke-dashoffset:0; transform:rotate(180deg)}`, icon `opacity:.3`, `--speed:10ms` `[CSS]`. Depo Luxe's link underline is a `currentColor` hairline that *appears* rather than growing:

```css
.link-underline:before { background-color:currentColor; height:.5px;
                         bottom:var(--bottom-line); opacity:var(--opacity);
                         transition:.2s; width:100% }
.link-underline:hover  { --opacity:1; transition:.1s }
```

`--bottom-line` defaults to `.8em`. **Where it fits.** Photographic or 3D surfaces, where any fill would muddy them.

**d. Spotlight-dim siblings (list/nav focus).** Hovering one item drops its siblings, not itself. Son Daven's fullscreen menu holds the hovered item at `opacity:1` and drops all others to `0.2` over `durM 0.8s` "Out" `[JS]`. Depo Luxe's selected-works index swaps the row's metadata outright:

```css
.block-selected-works>ul a:hover{--opacity:1;--opacity-figure:.3}
.block-selected-works>ul a:hover .client,
.block-selected-works>ul a:hover .counter{opacity:0;transform:translateY(0);
  transition:transform .4s var(--ease-out-quad),opacity .1s 0s var(--ease-out-quad)}
.block-selected-works>ul a:hover .title{opacity:1;transition:opacity .4s var(--ease-in-quad)}
.block-selected-works>ul a:hover .client2,
.block-selected-works>ul a:hover .director{opacity:1;transform:translateY(0);
  transition:opacity .2s .05s var(--ease-in-quad)}
```

Client and counter fade out, the title brightens, director and secondary client fade in with a translate, and the paired figure dims to `.3`. A row-level metadata reveal, not a text decoration. **Where it fits.** Nav lists and indexes carrying the primary interaction.

**e. Per-char scramble on nav items** `(single-source)`. Son Daven's `initNavItemHover`: `[hover-nav-item]` holds two `[hover='text']` copies split to chars; on enter the outgoing chars run `{opacity:1, yPercent:0, scale:1} → {opacity:0, yPercent:-75, scale:0}` and the incoming mirror it, `duration:durM(.8)`, `ease:"Out"`, `stagger:{each:.025, from:"random"}`, desktop-only via `min-width:992` matchMedia `[JS]`. Letters scatter in random order, not a clean roll.

**f. Nav-item color cross-fade plus micro-marker.** Delvaux nav links cross-fade `color 1s cubic-bezier(.65,0,.35,1)` between ink `#1d1d1b` and oxblood `#6f0b0b`; the open mega-menu fades in a 2px ink bullet at `left:-7px` (`opacity .4s cubic-bezier(.25,1,.5,1)`) rather than an underline `[CSS]`. **Where it fits.** Navs held whisper-quiet.

**g. Image and card restraint: 1.02–1.05× zoom, long.** Brunello editorial media takes `scale(1.05)` over `transform 1.5s cubic-bezier(.165,.84,.44,1)` (easeOutQuint); product cards `scale(1.05)` guarded by `@media (hover:hover)` `[CSS]`. Delvaux's *only* hover zoom in 318KB of CSS is `scale(1.02)` on a color swatch; no product-grid zoom exists `[CSS]`, restraint as identity. **Where it fits.** Zooms of 1.02–1.05× over 0.8–1.5s; never a fast 1.1× snap.

**Nav-bar surface.** Four winners, four surfaces, zero contrasting border-bottoms:

- **Section's own color, faded in on scroll.** Son Daven `.header_bg` holds `opacity:0` until scroll > 1000px, then fades in the *current section's* token (`#a89474` over light, `#2c2824` over dark); the header hides on scroll-down and returns on scroll-up. No border, no blur `[JS/CSS]`.
- **Opaque plate plus a near-invisible same-hue hairline.** Delvaux `.header-navigation-background`: `background:#fff`, `border-bottom:1px solid rgba(29,29,27,.03)`, a 3%-opacity *same-ink* hairline, effectively invisible. No `backdrop-filter`, no `mix-blend-mode` `[CSS]`.
- **Photographic gradient scrim, no fill.** Brunello `.c-site-header` is `position:fixed; pointer-events:none` with a top-down gradient PNG (`header-gradient…png`, `height:250%`) behind it: no solid bar, no border, no CSS blur `[CSS]`.
- **Low-opacity palette-flipping blur tint.** Depo Luxe `#Header__blur`: `backdrop-filter:blur(40px)` over a 5% tint that flips with the section (`rgba(0,0,0,.05)` / `rgba(255,255,255,.05)`, `.2s ease-out`); no border-bottom `[CSS]`.

The scrolled nav takes the section's own color (opaque, or as a low blurred tint) or a photographic scrim. The hairline, where one exists, is the text hue at a few percent opacity.

### Motion and scroll

The AI-default: everything fades up 20px on a generic `IntersectionObserver` with `ease-out 0.6s`, whole-page hero parallax, the same reveal on headings, images, and cards alike.

Winners smooth-scroll everywhere, fire `ScrollTrigger` reveals `once:true`, and, as the signature, use **clip-path and mask reveals rather than opacity fades**, on a small shared easing lexicon.

**a. Smooth scroll as the substrate.** Lenis on Son Daven and Brunello `[JS]`; GSAP ScrollSmoother on Delvaux `[JS]`; a bespoke lerped wheel scroll on Depo Luxe (`lerp(p0,-p1,t)`) `[JS]`; Lenis on Cartier (case study). Universal on this line.

**b. Masked line and word reveal, the house reveal.** Text and media enter from behind a hard `overflow:hidden` or `clip-path` mask, never a soft fade. Delvaux headline: SplitText lines, each masked, `y:0, duration:2, stagger:.2, power4.inOut` with a slower `duration:3, power4.out` container drift under it `[JS]`; media wipe `clip-path:inset(0 0 0 0)` over `1s cubic-bezier(.25,1,.5,1)` `[CSS]`. Son Daven divider lines wipe `clip-path:inset(0% 100% -1px 0%) → inset(0% 0% -1px 0%)`, `durL 1.2s` "Out" `[JS]`. Depo Luxe `[data-mask-child]` animates a `clip-path:polygon(...)` inset from CSS vars `[CSS]`.

**c. Modest parallax and horizontal headline shear.** Amplitudes stay small. Son Daven images `yPercent -20→20` (`scrub`, `ease:none`); H1 child words drift `xPercent wrap([5,-1,-5]) → wrap([-5,1,5])` (`scrub:1`) so display lines shear sideways at different rates `[JS]`. Depo Luxe `[data-scroll-displace]` on 16 elements, container `overflow:hidden` `[CSS]`. The vertical differential stays at or below 20%; the sideways headline shear is the fresher alternative.

**d. Clip-path wipe as the universal transition grammar.** Beyond reveals, menus, overlays, and sections open and close with the same `clip-path:inset()` primitive. Delvaux's mega-menu opens from `inset(0 0 100% 0)`; overlays close to `inset(0 0 0 100%)` over `1s cubic-bezier(.25,1,.5,1)` behind a `backdrop-filter:blur(4px)` and a 10%-ink scrim `[CSS]`. Depo Luxe's section `palette-primary ↔ secondary` inversion swaps the whole black/white scheme on scroll `[CSS]`. One reveal primitive carries reveals, menus, and overlays alike.

**e. Scroll-scrubbed canvas or frame sequence** `(single-source, pattern-typical of the 3D sub-line)`. Son Daven scrubs a `<canvas>` through a preloaded image-frame array: `snap:"frame"`, `scrub:0.25`, `start:"top top" → "75% bottom"` `[JS]`. Cartier and Longines drive a scroll-controlled camera through 3D scenes with a transition-to-WebGL hand-off from DOM into canvas (studio and award prose).

**Verified easing lexicon.** Use these, not a generic `ease-out`. The easeOutQuart curve appears independently on two builds, which makes it a better default for this archetype than the `cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)` usually cited as the luxury ease.

| Curve | Name and role | Where read |
|---|---|---|
| `cubic-bezier(0.25, 1, 0.5, 1)` | easeOutQuart, the workhorse reveal curve | Son Daven `CustomEase "Out"` and Delvaux `revealEase` (×103 in the entry CSS), arrived at independently `[JS/CSS]` |
| `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` | easeOutExpo, snappier tail | Brunello Cucinelli `[CSS, sibling build]` |
| `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)` | easeInOutQuart, symmetric moves | Delvaux label roll and nav color cross-fade (×96) `[CSS]` |
| `cubic-bezier(0.76, 0, 0.24, 1)` | "InOut", menu and section transitions | Son Daven `[JS]` |
| `cubic-bezier(0.5, 0, 0.75, 0)` | "In" | Son Daven `[JS]`; Delvaux ×67 `[CSS]` |
| `cubic-bezier(0.333, 0, 0.667, 1)` | "Write", the scrubbed reading emphasis | Son Daven `[JS]` |
| `cubic-bezier(0.165, 0.84, 0.44, 1)` | easeOutQuint, slow image zoom | Brunello `[CSS]` |
| Full quad/expo family as `--ease-*` vars | reveals easeOutQuad, fades easeInQuad | Depo Luxe `[CSS]` |

**Verified durations.** Label roll `0.2s`; nav color fade `1s`; line/word reveal `1.0–1.2s`; masked headline `2–3s`; media clip wipe `1.0–1.5s`; image hover zoom `1.5s`; smooth-scroll lerp `~1.2s`. Son Daven codifies constants `durL=1.2 / durM=.8 / durS=.4`, `stagger=.1`, `delayReveal=.2`, `breakPoint=992`. Delvaux codifies `--animation-t1..t6 = 1.5 / 1.3 / 1.0 / 0.7 / 0.4 / 0.2s`.

### Text effects

The serif is the mark (all display type in the corpus is serif) and it enters via masked line or word reveals, never per-letter typing. Long durations, `power4` and quart eases.

**a. Masked per-line rise, the signature.** SplitText into lines, each wrapped in an `overflow:hidden` mask, lines sliding up behind the clip. Delvaux: `duration:2, stagger:.2, power4.inOut` plus a `duration:3, power4.out` container lift underneath `[JS]`. Son Daven paragraphs: lines `yPercent 250→0`, `durL 1.2s`, `stagger .05`, "Out" `[JS]`. Depo Luxe: SplitText lines under `.line-parent{overflow:hidden}` `[JS/CSS]`. The archetype's default headline reveal.

**b. Word scatter-in, the playful variation** `(single-source)`. Son Daven headlines split into **words**, each starting `scale:0, opacity:0` at alternating `yPercent wrap([-150, 75, -75, 150])`, some dropping, some rising, at varied distances, assembling over `durL 1.2s` "Out" with `stagger:{each:.025, from:"random"}` `[JS]`. **Where it fits.** Display headings on a brand with warmth or craft.

**c. Clip-wipe divider lines.** Hairline rules draw in left to right, `clip-path:inset(0% 100% -1px 0%) → inset(0% 0% -1px 0%)`, `durL`, "Out" `[JS]`. Supporting, not signature.

**d. Scroll-scrubbed reading emphasis** `(single-source)`. Son Daven's `initHighlightText`; see Mid-page aliveness, where it carries the line's answer to keeping prose alive.

**e. Roman-numeral and oldstyle-figure micro-type.** Editorial credibility in the small type: Depo Luxe indexes works via CSS `counter(roman-counter, upper-roman)` `[CSS]`; Delvaux's display serif carries `font-variant-numeric:oldstyle-nums` plus `small-caps` `[CSS]`. Reinforces the inherited-not-chosen voice.

**Display serifs in the corpus.** KTF Metro Roman (Son Daven, Kyiv Type Foundry, with `"Times New Roman"` fallback; body is KTF Metro Blueline with Arial fallback, both wired through `--_fonts---font-display` / `--_fonts---font-body`), Genath (Delvaux, with Atlas Grotesk for body), FreightBig Pro (Brunello, via Typekit `vbi4iuh.css` alongside freight-text-pro), EB Garamond (Depo Luxe, self-hosted woff2 under `--font-serif` / `--font-serif-italic`), engraved 3D serif (Cartier). All carry tight negative tracking at display; Son Daven headings run `letter-spacing:-0.064em`.

### Cursor and pointer

Two coherent poles: **native cursor plus magnetic pull** on editorial and e-commerce sites, **a minimal custom cursor confined to the WebGL surface** on experiential ones. No oversized blob anywhere.

**a. Native cursor plus magnetic pull, no follower.** Son Daven ships **no** cursor element. `[data-magnetic-strength]` elements translate toward the pointer (`offset · strength / 16` em, `power4.out`, `duration:1.6`) and spring back with `elastic.out(1, 0.3)`, `duration:1.6`, desktop-only (`≤991` returns early), with a nested `[data-magnetic-inner-target]` `[JS]`. The springy overshoot on release is the tell; magnetic is applied to circular CTAs only. Delvaux has **no** custom or magnetic cursor at all: native plus Swiper grab `[CSS/JS]`.

**b. Custom cursor confined to canvas.** Brunello hides the native cursor only over the WebGL hero (`.ep-banner-overlay{cursor:none}`) and carries a frosted `.tooltip-mouse` label (`backdrop-filter:blur(30px)`, warm-white gradient) `[CSS]`. Cartier's bundle carries a custom cursor (38 references) for its 3D scenes `[JS]`.

**c. Attribute-driven modal cursor** `(single-source)`. Depo Luxe's Cuchillo cursor is a `position:fixed` element with modes `data-cursor-{magnetic,drag,follow,image,icon,text,color,rotation}`: magnetic, drag affordance, or a text label or icon. The native cursor is hidden only in the fullscreen video takeover (`cursor:none`, ×3 in the CSS), and a `.--diff{mix-blend-mode:difference}` utility applies to the dot, not a page-wide blob `[JS/CSS]`.

**d. Grab cursors for draggable media.** Swiper and gallery sliders set `grab` → `grabbing` (Son Daven, Delvaux, Brunello) `[CSS/JS]`.

### Loader effects

The choice is content-driven: asset-heavy 3D and WebGL sites gate on *real* load progress; a pure-CSS editorial site paints instantly. No fake timers.

**a. Real-progress counter tied to asset load.** Son Daven's `[data-preloader-percent]` counts `0% → 100%` via `Math.round(loaded/total·100)`, gated on `Promise.all([frames, WebGL scenes ready, fonts.ready])`; an intro video scales `1.5 → 1` over `2.4s` "Out"; the logo **Flips** from the preloader into the header slot (`1.8s` "InOut"); scroll stays locked until done, first visit only via `sessionStorage` `[JS]`. Cartier's bundle carries a loader-plus-percent system (124 `loader` / 16 `percent` references) `[JS]`. Brunello ships a percent/progress preloader `[JS]`.

**b. Progress-keyed logo reveal, no number.** Depo Luxe's `#Preloader` (white background, black serif, uppercase) reveals an SVG logo whose `path{opacity:0}` fills in as an asset loader (`itemsLoaded/itemsTotal`) advances; progress reads through the mark, not a counter. The markup carries a `video-preloader` plus a `preloader__logo` of 3 `<svg>` and 12 `<path>` `[CSS/JS]`.

**c. Instant paint plus scroll reveals, no intro screen.** Delvaux has **no** fullscreen preloader and **no** percentage counter, only 8px async spinners for cart and data, and Nuxt's thin `nuxt-loading-indicator` in the static markup. Entrance motion is the scroll and clip reveal system `[CSS]`. For a fast editorial DOM with no heavy 3D payload, a gate only adds latency.

**d. Logo-Flip hand-off** `(single-source)`. The preloader logo does not cut: it Flips into its final header position, so the intro resolves *into* the layout (Son Daven, `1.8s` "InOut") `[JS]`.

### Composition

Coherence by **grammar**, not by repetition: one easing curve and one reveal primitive reused at every scale and axis, while each element class gets a distinct geometry. Variety in what moves, unity in how it moves.

**Delvaux: one curve, one primitive, four geometries.**

| Element class | Move |
|---|---|
| CTA | label roll on Y behind an `overflow:hidden` clip, no color — `translateY(±100%)` |
| Headline | line rise on Y behind a mask, same clip mechanism at larger scale (`power4.inOut`, dur 2) |
| Image / section media | clip-path inset wipe on X |
| Menu / overlays | clip-path inset open/close on X — same primitive as the media wipe |
| Nav item | color cross-fade plus a 2px bullet dot, the one non-clip move |
| Nav bar | opaque white plate plus a 3%-ink hairline |

The coherence engine is a *primary* easing, `revealEase = cubic-bezier(0.25,1,0.5,1)`, byte-identical in the CSS custom property and the GSAP `CustomEase`, ×103 in the entry CSS, carrying one reveal grammar (reveal-from-behind-a-clip) applied at every scale. It is a lineage, not a monopoly: the symmetric moves run `cubic-bezier(.65,0,.35,1)` (label roll `.2s`, nav color cross-fade `1s`, ×96), an in-curve `cubic-bezier(.5,0,.75,0)` appears ×67, and the masked headline is GSAP `power4.inOut` (`duration 2, stagger .2`) over a `power4.out` container drift. What never changes is the primitive; the curve moves inside a small measured family. Restraint is itself the signature: 2% maximum image zoom, no cursor, no fill hovers.

**Son Daven: one easing family plus one connective texture.**

| Element class | Move |
|---|---|
| Nav item | per-char random scramble, dual-copy |
| Menu | focus-dim siblings to `0.2` |
| CTA (pill) | invert to the full section token plus a staged label roll |
| CTA (circular) | magnetic pull with elastic release |
| Headline | word scatter-in from alternating `yPercent` |
| Paragraph | masked line rise |
| Image | parallax `yPercent ±20` plus reveal through a hand-written WebGL dot-grid/embroidery shader |
| Nav bar | current section's own token, faded in past 1000px, hide-on-scroll-down |

Everything runs on the same five CustomEase curves (chiefly "Out" = `cubic-bezier(0.25,1,0.5,1)`) and the same `durL/durM/durS` constants; a random-order stagger motif recurs across nav chars and headline words; and a single dot-grid shader renders every photographic scene (hero, seasons, prolog, fin, preloader), so wildly different interactions read as one hand. The theme-inversion system reassigns the two-token palette on scroll, so the same rules read correctly on light and dark sections.

**The transferable rule.** Each site runs one easing (easeOutQuart `cubic-bezier(0.25,1,0.5,1)` is the corpus default) and one reveal primitive (masked translate or clip-path inset). Every element class gets its own geometry: Y-roll for buttons, Y-rise for headlines, X-wipe for media, opacity-dim for lists, magnetic for circular CTAs. No second curve or second primitive appears without a reason in the brief, and one connective texture (a shader, a grain, a palette-inversion system) touches every surface. That is how five interactions read as one voice instead of one interaction read five times.

### Anti-signals — absent from every winner examined

- **Pale or washed-out tint fill on button hover.** Zero winners. They roll the label, invert to a full solid token, draw a line, or do nothing.
- **Contrasting-accent `border-bottom` under the scrolled nav.** Zero winners.
- **One uniform hover repeated across all element classes.** Zero winners.
- **Generic fade-up-20px-on-scroll for everything.** Replaced by masked line and word reveals and clip-path inset wipes.
- **Per-letter typewriter as the headline reveal.** Zero winners; masked *line* or *word* reveals only.
- **Fake timed spinner or fixed-duration progress bar.** Loaders tie to real asset progress or a progress-keyed logo reveal, or the site paints instantly.
- **Large custom cursor blob with grow-on-hover and page-wide mix-blend.** Custom cursors are minimal and scoped to the WebGL surface.
- **Frosted-white glassmorphism nav as a default.** Two builds use no blur at all (Son Daven, Delvaux); the two that blur do it at a low palette-flipping tint or a gradient scrim.
- **Neon or high-saturation accent.** Palettes are two-tone warm-neutral (Son Daven `#a89474` / `#2c2824`; Brunello almond / `#262626`) or monochrome (Depo Luxe `#000` / `#fff`). The one saturated accent, Delvaux oxblood `#6f0b0b`, is muted and reserved for a seasonal theme and the nav-hover color.
- **Fast 1.1×+ image pop on hover.** Zoom is 1.02–1.05× over 0.8–1.5s, or absent.

## Page recipe

Three shapes hold this line, each defined by how the argument is delivered: shown as a campaign, argued across a long scroll, or staged inside an engine.

### Named page shapes

#### Shape A — Editorial maison scroll

**Fingerprint.** A warm neutral foundation, custom serif display, and a rhythm of full-bleed campaign frames alternating with asymmetric image-text editorial blocks. No percentage-counter loader: the DOM paints instantly and the scroll reveals are the entrance. The first fold is a fullscreen hero, a slider or a single campaign frame, never a product grid. Commerce hides behind "Discover"; the close is a boutique or appointment invitation plus a tabular mega-footer carrying the maison tagline. Intensity is even and low-key; this line rests rather than climaxes, and the confidence *is* the restraint.

**Ordered skeleton (Delvaux, live).** Intensity `[n]` is a comparative 1–10 estimate of attention load (defined in [Composition Chains](../winners/composition-chains.md)). `fullscreen home-slider hero (slide-visual + overlay scrim + serif slide-title + roll-swap/stroke CTAs) [7] → triple-image editorial swiper ("Explore what's new") [5] → dual-image editorial swiper (emblematic product "Le Brillant, the one and only" + "Boutique Appointment") [6] → newsletter + accordion/region tabular footer [3, rest]`. Roughly 4–6 viewport-heights. Class evidence: `home-slider__slide-visual` / `-overlay` / `-title-line`, `triple-image__*` (28 hits), `dual-image__*`, `footer__newsletter-holder` / `-nav-accordion-holder` / `-region-holder`.

**Fits.** Fashion houses, fragrance, fine leather goods and jewelry, premium lifestyle DTC with a campaign to sell. Brunello Cucinelli's `.com` philosophy site is the story-first, non-commerce variant.

#### Shape B — Argument long-scroll

**Fingerprint.** A content-driven preloader, dark, with scene labels, hands via GSAP Flip into an eyebrow-plus-display hero, then a long editorial scroll arguing a full case: lyrical prologue, concept, a guided tour of the offering, an infrastructure and location map, financials, developer proof, editorial, a named close CTA, an FAQ, and a contact-first footer. The shape when the maison has to persuade, not just seduce.

**Ordered skeleton (Son Daven, live).** `master-preloader (scene labels) → Flip into hero (eyebrow "investment project" + display "Design Resort Hotel") [7] → prologue quote [6] → About / Concept [5] → guided place tour ("where the mountains Speak" → Waterfall → Park of legends "Gedzo" → Hutsulshchyna → Dovbucha Rocks → Carpathians in miniature → souvenir market) [7, each landmark a beat] → Infrastructure walk-times + Location map [6] → Apartments [5] → Financial indicators [6] → Developer proof (20+/2/37/7 stat row) [5] → Blog [4] → "Become part of the legend" CTA [7, climax] → FAQ [4] → contact footer (Sales departments by city) [3, rest]`. Fifteen-plus sections, the deepest scroll in the corpus.

**Fits.** Premium real estate and developments, luxury hospitality and resorts, watchmaking and automotive launches carrying an investment or heritage thesis.

#### Shape C — Cinematic engine (pavilion or folio)

**Fingerprint.** A WebGL or real-time substrate carries the experience, gated by a content-driven loader that resolves *into* the first scene with no hard cut, scored by a bespoke soundscape, driven by gesture and scroll. Two sub-variants. **C-a Pavilion** stages refined 3D universes the camera glides through (Cartier: alcoves with display cases, gesture reveals of timepiece detail). **C-b Folio** stages a monochrome cinematic index, a single lyrical statement over a WebGL-displaced field, a section index, and audio (Depo Luxe: production-studio register, video-preloader plus SVG-logo fill). Palette collapses to two-tone or monochrome; the custom cursor is scoped, not a page-wide blob.

**Ordered skeleton (Cartier pavilion, shipped/case-study).** `in-engine content loader → intro resolves into universe 1 → scroll/gesture camera glide between refined 3D universes (content pages ↔ product interaction ↔ 3D universes) [climax = gesture-revealed timepiece detail, scored] → in-world sign-off`. Non-linear; depth lives inside each universe.

**Ordered skeleton (Depo Luxe folio, live).** `video-preloader + preloader__logo SVG reveal → H1 statement "A strategic and cinematic approach to contemporary luxury" over WebGL field [7] → nav-index sections (Featured reel → Archive → Talent → Approach → Contact) [6, spotlight-dim on hover] → signed oversized-logo footer [3]`. One to two folds of chrome; depth lives in each opened section.

**Fits.** Luxury launches, watchmaking, jewelry openings, automotive concept reveals, premium architecture and hotels (pavilion); creative-production studios, talent agencies, luxury folios (folio).

**Totals across the line.** Shape A: 4–6 viewport-heights, no climax spike. Shape B: 15+ sections, single late climax at the close CTA. Shape C: 1–2 folds of chrome, climax on gesture or open. Rest is always a tabular, contact, or signed footer, never a spectacle footer.

## Mid-page aliveness

What keeps the middle of the page alive: the prose and content zone between hero and footer that reads dead on merely-good 7.2–7.4 builds.

### The three welded channels

The middle stays alive through three channels, not by decorating the prose: a fire-once reveal on every content block as it enters, a reversible scroll-scrubbed decor layer running continuously underneath (parallax, WebGL grade, camera, displaced footage), and a small idle touch (marquee, sound, magnetic CTA). The prose itself is mostly revealed, then left still, with one telling exception.

**Son Daven** `(winner-verified)`, the fullest kit. The custom module wires roughly 135 `data-scroll-reveal` elements plus a dozen named `init*` systems. Timing constants `durL=1.2s`, `durM=.8s`, `durS=.4s`, `stagger=.1`, `delayReveal=.2`, `breakPoint=992`. CustomEase set: `Out=0.25,1,0.5,1`, `In=0.5,0,0.75,0`, `InOut=0.76,0,0.24,1`, `Write=0.333,0,0.667,1`, plus an unused `Ease=0.25,0.1,0.25,1`.

- **Content reveals, fire-once** (`initScrollElementsReveal`). `data-scroll-reveal="h|p|ctn|line"`, each `ScrollTrigger.create({trigger:t||e, start:"top bottom", once:!0, onEnter})`. Two primitives: headings (`animateTextH`) split to **words**, `fromTo({yPercent:wrap([-150,75,-75,150]), scale:0, opacity:0} → {yPercent:0, scale:1, opacity:1}, duration:durL, stagger:{each:.25*stagger, from:"random"}, ease:"Out"})`, where `.25*stagger` resolves to `.025`; paragraphs (`animateTextP`) split to **lines**, `fromTo({yPercent:250, opacity:0} → {yPercent:0, opacity:1}, duration:durL, stagger:.5*stagger, ease:"Out"})`, resolving to `.05`.
- **The one live text effect, a scrubbed reading-highlight** (`initHighlightText`). `[data-highlight-text]` splits to chars (`split-char`), then `gsap.timeline({scrollTrigger:{trigger, start:"top 75%", end:"bottom 50%", scrub:!0}}).from(chars, {opacity:.1, duration:durS, ease:"Out", stagger:stagger})`. Characters wash from `opacity:.1` to full as the block travels 75% → 50% of the viewport, and un-wash on scroll-up. The single technique on the whole line that animates body text *continuously* rather than once.
- **Idle marquee** (`initMarquee`). `[data-marquee="list"]`, `timeline({repeat:-1}).to(list,{xPercent:-100, duration:24, ease:"linear"})`, wrapped in a ScrollTrigger that plays and pauses on enter and leave, with `onUpdate: timeScale(1 + .01*getVelocity())`: the marquee speeds and drags with scroll velocity.
- **Magnetic CTAs** (`initMagneticEffect`). `[data-magnetic-strength]`, desktop-only, spring-back `ease:"elastic.out(1, 0.3)", duration:1.6`, with a nested `[data-magnetic-inner-target]`.
- **Scroll-displaced film** (`initScrollVideo`). `gsap.to({frame:0}, {frame:n.length-1, snap:"frame", ease:"none", scrollTrigger:{start:"top top", end:"75% bottom", scrub:.25}})`: footage advances and reverses with the wheel.
- **Section transitions** (`initSectionTransition`). Scrubbed scale ramps (`.img-w` `scale:1→1.25`), a pinned horizontal product procession (`.benefits-cms_list` `xPercent:-100` with `containerAnimation` reparenting item rotation and position), and a footer scaling in from `scale:2`. All scrubbed, all reversible.
- **Parallax bed** (`initAllParallax`). Five scrubbed layers: `[parallax="img"]` `yPercent -20→20`, `[parallax="img-out"]` `-10→30`, `[parallax="ctn-down"]` / `[parallax="ctn-up"]` ±10, and `[parallax="h1"]` a per-word horizontal drift `xPercent:wrap([5,-1,-5]) → wrap([-5,1,5])`. All `ease:"none"`, all scrubbed.
- **WebGL scene grade.** `initSceneHeroOver/Bg`, `initSceneBenefitsIntro/Outro`, `initSceneDevOver/Bg` drive canvas `blackPoint` (×61), `whitePoint` (×60), and `threshold` (×60), every one scrubbed to a ScrollTrigger.

**Delvaux** `(winner-verified)`, the quiet register, almost entirely fire-once. The editorial maison keeps the middle alive with reveals and smoothing only: no marquee, no scrubbed text, no idle loop anywhere in 318KB of CSS. The only infinite animations are loader, spinner, Plyr, Swiper, and preload.

Dual-image variants run `2s`; clip-path wipes run `1s` (×3) and `1.5s` (×1) on the same curve, `inset(0 0 100% 0) → inset(0 0 0 0)`. Reveals are gated `once:!0` (×17) with `.batch(` ×2. The decor layer is GSAP ScrollSmoother (`effects:iw`, `normalizeScroll` ×2, zero Lenis): reversible, continuous, gentle, and the whole of Delvaux's reversible motion. Between reveals the page is still, consistent with the Honorable Mention: the quietest register reads as least alive to the jury even at impeccable craft.

**Depo Luxe** `(winner-verified)`, WebGL folio. **Cartier** `(shipped/case-study)`, the whole middle scrubbed WebGL. There is no dead prose zone because there is barely any prose.

### Hover on text

The tier ships hover responses on *interactive* text registers (nav items, index rows, links) and essentially none on non-interactive prose. No heading, body paragraph, or standalone number gets a hover state. **No site changes `font-weight`, `font-variation-settings`, or `font-stretch` on any hover**: zero across the Delvaux CSS, the Depo Luxe CSS, and the Son Daven module.

The forms that do ship are the per-char nav roll-swap and menu spotlight-dim (Son Daven), the index-row metadata cross-fade and underline-appear (Depo Luxe), all quoted in the Effect palette above, plus Delvaux's entire text-hover register: `.footer__nav-list a:hover{opacity:.7}`, `.--underline:hover{opacity:.7}`, and `.color-selector__link:hover .color-selector__image{transform:scale(1.02)}`. Nothing on a heading or a line of prose.

Hovering non-link body copy or a display heading is not what this tier does. The hover budget goes to nav, index, and links. Making *prose* feel alive is a scroll effect, not a hover.

### Re-fire behavior

The split is clean across the tier.

**Fires once and stays (content).** Son Daven's every `data-scroll-reveal` uses `once:!0`; Delvaux's `[data-title-reveal]`, `[data-image-reveal]`, and batch reveals all use `once:!0`; Depo Luxe and Brunello gate reveals once. Nothing re-animates on a second pass.

**Re-plays every pass (decor, welded to scroll).** Parallax: Son Daven's five layers, Delvaux's ScrollSmoother effects, Depo Luxe's VirtualScroll `data-speed`, all `ease:"none"`, all scrubbed. Scrubbed spectacle: Son Daven's frame-sequence film, pinned product procession, and footer scale-in; Depo Luxe's WebGL-displaced footage; Cartier's scrubbed camera. WebGL grade shifts: Son Daven's per-scene `blackPoint` / `whitePoint` / `threshold` timelines.

**The one text exception that proves the rule.** Son Daven's `initHighlightText` char-opacity wash runs `scrub:!0`. It re-plays on every pass because it is *decor-register*, an emphasis wash over reading, not a content reveal. A per-line masked *reveal* welded to scrub would violate the law; an opacity *wash* welded to scrub honors it.

### Smooth scroll

Wheel smoothing is universal at this tier, 5 of 5, but the library is not standardized.

- **Son Daven** `(winner-verified)`. `new Lenis({wrapper:window, duration:1.2, smoothWheel:!0, touchMultiplier:2, easing:e=>Math.min(1,1.001-Math.pow(2,-10*e)), infinite:!1})`, driven off `gsap.ticker` with `lagSmoothing(0)` and `lenis.on("scroll", ScrollTrigger.update)`. A nested Lenis at `duration:.6` runs `[data-lenis-scroll]` inner panels.
- **Brunello** `(shipped, sibling build)`. Lenis bundled behind a ReactLenis wrapper with `smoothWheel`; the duration resolves to the library default, not an authored value.
- **Delvaux** `(winner-verified)`. GSAP ScrollSmoother (`effects`, `normalizeScroll`), not Lenis. Same felt result, different toolchain.
- **Depo Luxe** `(winner-verified)`. A custom VirtualScroll synced to the WebGL frame loop, `progress`-driven, reading `data-speed` for parallax.
- **Cartier** `(shipped/case-study)`. Lenis and GSAP on the same nervous system.

Smoothing is a floor, not a differentiator. Lenis is the majority default; ScrollSmoother is the sanctioned alternative when GSAP already carries the parallax; a hand-rolled virtual scroll is expected once a WebGL surface needs scroll and render on one clock.

### Mid-page anti-signals

- **No hover on prose or headings.** Zero weight-shift, zero `font-variation-settings` sweep, zero per-char rise on body copy or display type.
- **No fast image pop.** Reveals run 1.5–2s, hover zoom is 1.02–1.05× over long durations.
- **No re-animating content.** Reveals are `once:!0` everywhere.
- **No generic fade-up-20px for everything.** Each element class gets its own geometry on one shared curve, easeOutQuart `cubic-bezier(0.25,1,0.5,1)`, arrived at independently by Son Daven and Delvaux.
- **No per-letter typewriter.** Masked per-line and per-word reveals only; the one per-*char* system is the scrubbed opacity wash, not a sequential type-on.
- **No decorative idle in the quiet register.** Delvaux ships no marquee, no ambient loop, no scrubbed text; stillness between reveals is deliberate. Where idle motion appears it is welded to scroll velocity or to narrative, never free-floating.

### Keeping the middle alive

The merely-good build reads dead in its prose sections because it treats aliveness as decorating each paragraph. The 7.6-tier does the opposite: it **reveals each content block once and then leaves it still, while a reversible decor layer runs continuously underneath the whole scroll**. Three moves, in priority order:

1. **A reversible decor bed under the entire mid-page.** `data-speed` parallax on media (cheapest, universal), a scroll-scrubbed WebGL grade or displacement on the footage, or a scrubbed frame-sequence film. This is what makes the *space between* prose blocks feel alive: the reader's hand is always moving something and never re-triggering content. Welded to scroll, `ease:"none"`, reverses on scroll-up.
2. **One fire-once reveal primitive per element class, on one curve.** Words scatter in for headings, lines slide from `yPercent:250` behind a mask for paragraphs, images translate `20%` or clip-wipe for media, all `once:!0`, all easeOutQuart, `durL ≈ 1.2s`. Distinct geometry, shared curve.
3. **For prose itself, a scroll-scrubbed reading emphasis, not a hover.** SplitText to chars, `scrub` a `from(opacity:.1)` wash across `top 75% → bottom 50%`. It re-plays because it is decor over reading; hover stays on nav, index, and links.

Restraint is the register, and it costs Usability points on Awwwards no matter how well executed. The ceiling stays at ~7.64 (see The score reality); the target is the craft, not the score.
